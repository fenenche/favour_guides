English Language

A guide covering grammar, comprehension, and essay writing for junior secondary to high school students.

Grammar Basics

Good grammar starts with knowing the basic building blocks of a sentence: nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs. A sentence needs at least a subject (who or what) and a verb (the action) to be complete.

Common mistakes to watch for: mixing up "their," "there," and "they're"; subject-verb agreement (e.g. "he goes" not "he go"); and using the correct verb tense consistently throughout a piece of writing.

Reading Comprehension

When answering comprehension questions, always read the passage once fully before looking at the questions. Then read the questions, and go back to find the exact part of the passage that answers each one.

Answer using your own words where possible, but if a question asks for a specific fact or definition, it's fine to quote directly from the passage. Always answer in full sentences unless told otherwise.

Essay Writing

A good essay has three main parts: an introduction that states your main point, body paragraphs that each cover one idea with supporting details, and a conclusion that sums up your argument.

Before writing, spend a few minutes planning: list your main points in the order you'll cover them. This makes your essay easier to follow and keeps you from repeating yourself.
