Science

Core concepts in biology, chemistry, and physics for junior secondary to high school students.

Biology Basics

Living things are made of cells, the basic building blocks of life. Plant and animal cells share some features, like a nucleus and cell membrane, but plant cells also have a cell wall and chloroplasts for photosynthesis.

Photosynthesis is how plants make their own food, using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to produce glucose and oxygen. This process is the foundation of most food chains on Earth.

Chemistry Basics

Everything around us is made of matter, which is built from atoms. Atoms combine to form molecules, and different combinations of elements create the substances we see and use every day.

The periodic table organizes all known elements by their properties. Learning the first 20 elements and their symbols (like H for Hydrogen, O for Oxygen) is a great starting point for chemistry.

Physics Basics

Physics explains how matter and energy interact. One key idea is Newton's Laws of Motion, which describe how objects move and respond to forces — for example, an object stays at rest or in motion unless a force acts on it.

Energy comes in different forms, like kinetic (motion), potential (stored), and thermal (heat), and it can change from one form to another but is never created or destroyed.
